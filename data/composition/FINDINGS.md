# What is already established, and what is already known to be blocked

Four research passes went into this before the table was started. Everything they
confirmed is here with its verbatim quote, so nobody re-fetches it — and so is the list
of sources that are hard-blocked, which is the more valuable half. Read both before
opening a browser.

Grades are the ones in [`SCHEMA.md`](SCHEMA.md): **standard**, **trade**, **inferred**,
**contested**.

## Confirmed correspondences

### Forequarter

**Flat iron / top blade / 부채살 / ミスジ = `infraspinatus`** — trade
> `Infraspinatus` — Common Name: "Flat iron, Book steak, Top Blade"
> — <https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/5>

> "Infraspinatus | Top blade; Flat Iron; Triangle"
> — Calkins & Sullivan, <https://www.paffa.org/system/ckeditor_assets/attachments/1146/66020_Beef_Muscle_Common_Names.pdf>

Korean trade agrees (<https://www.meatlab.co.kr/blogPost/grilling_theory_11>). **ko.wikipedia
disagrees**, naming 가시위근 (supraspinatus) for 부채살 — that looks like a transcription
error, since supraspinatus is 꾸리살; flag it, do not adopt it.

**Chuck tender / mock tender / トウガラシ / 꾸리살 = `supraspinatus`** — trade
> `Supraspinatus` — Common Name: "Mock tender, Chuck tender, Scotch tender, Chuck fillet"
> — <https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/20>

**Petite tender / shoulder tender = `teres-major`** — trade
> "Teres major | Shoulder Tender; Petite Tender" — Calkins & Sullivan (paffa.org PDF above)

> "**Major Muscles:** Teres major" — <https://ramcountrymeats.colostate.edu/product/beef-petite-tender/>

UNL has a `Teres major` entry with **no** Common Name field — do not cite UNL for this one.

**Denver steak / ザブトン / under blade = `serratus-ventralis`** — trade
> "**Major Muscles:** Serratus ventralis" — <https://ramcountrymeats.colostate.edu/product/beef-chuck-denver-steak/>

> "Serratus ventralis | Boneless short ribs; Inside chuck; Underblade" — Calkins & Sullivan

> `Serratus ventralis` — Common Name: "Under blade" — <https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/7>

Japanese: ザブトン given as 学名「Serratus ventralis」 —
<https://note.com/bridgeinter/n/na182e7935493>. Note UNL and Calkins never use the word
"Denver"; that link comes from CSU and the Japanese trade source.

**Paleron = `infraspinatus`** — trade
> Table 5 footnote: "MQ4 INSP : M. infraspinatus (paleron)"
> — <https://www.viandesetproduitscarnes.com/index.php/fr/process-et-technologies/323-evaluation-du-persille-et-des-qualites-sensorielles-des-carcasses-bovines>

> "le paleron (infraspinatus)" / EN abstract "oyster blade (m. infraspinatus)"
> — <https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122439/records/669e832f00eb85b7d72c0403>

So **paleron, not macreuse, is the flat-iron muscle.** fr.wikipedia's *Paleron* page says
it "correspond, chez l'homme, au muscle deltoïde" — a human analogy, not a bovine answer.

**Macreuse à bifteck (noix / boule de macreuse) = `triceps-brachii`, caput longum** — trade
> "MQ4 TBCL : M. triceps brachii caput longum (macreuse à bifteck)" — VPC 323, URL above

⚠ The Canadian *macreuse de palette* is a **different cut** (CFIA gives supraspinatus or
infraspinatus for it). Do not transfer Canadian nomenclature onto the French cut.

### Brisket — the pairing most often published inverted

**Brisket FLAT (IMPS 120A) = `pectoralis-profundus`; brisket POINT (120B) = `pectoralis-superficialis`** — standard
> deep pectoral is "located on the most interior (medial) surface of the brisket"; superficial
> pectoral is "located on the exterior (lateral) portion of the brisket"; "The two muscles …
> may also be separated by following the natural seam between the muscles to make a 120A Beef
> Brisket Flat and a 120B Beef Brisket Point."
> — <https://meat.tamu.edu/120-beef-brisket-deckle-off-boneless/>

UNL puts "Deckle" on **both** (<https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/85> and
`/86`) — quote them as written, they are ambiguous. NAV synonymy: *pectoralis profundus*
= *pectoralis ascendens*; the superficial group is *descendens* + *transversus*
(<https://www.imaios.com/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/pectoralis-profundus-muscle-pectoralis-ascendens-muscle-11077963240>).
TAMU = IMPS/English naming, UNL = Latin, IMAIOS = NAV. All three agree; only lay BBQ
blogs invert it.

### Loin and flank

**Faux-filet / contre-filet = `longissimus` + `gluteus-medius` + `multifidus`** — standard
> "**Contre-filet** (5.2.1.6) … Il renferme trois (3) muscles : le *gluteus medius*; le
> *longissimus dorsi*; et le *multifidus dorsi*."
> — <https://inspection.canada.ca/etiquetage-des-aliments/etiquetage/industrie/produits-de-viande-et-de-volaille/coupes-de-viande/boeuf/fra/1348582373896/1348589471777>

⚠ **In the same document**, Canadian *faux-filet* is the **rib** cut. Cite CFIA's
**contre-filet** entry for the French-French cut, never its *faux-filet* entry.

**Filet / tenderloin = `psoas-major`** (+ *m. iliacus*, unmodelled; `psoas-minor` is the
chain/side strap, in or out by trim spec) — standard
> "**Filet** (5.2.1.4): désigne le muscle principal (*psoas major* et *minor*) à forme
> cylindrique situé à l'intérieur (côté ventrale) de la **longe**." — CFIA, URL above

> "This cut is prepared from a full tenderloin (item 2454) by removing the side strap
> (m. psoas minor)." … "Only m. psoas major and m. iliacus remain in this cut."
> — <https://www.frigorificoforres.com.ar/en/productos/tenderloin-side-strap/>

**Bavette d'aloyau = `obliquus-internus-abdominis` — and it is US *flap meat*, NOT flank
steak** — standard (UNECE 2203)
> "internal flank plate is prepared from the flank and is the thickest portion of the
> m. obliquus internus abdominis muscle."
> — UNECE parallel text via <https://mymemory.translated.net/en/French/English/bavette-d-aloyau>

**Bavette de flanchet = `rectus-abdominis` — this is the US flank steak** — standard (UNECE 2210)
> "flank steak is prepared from a thin flank (2200) and is the flat lean fleshy portion of
> the m. rectus abdominis" — <https://mymemory.translated.net/en/French/English/bavette-de-flanchet>

**Fausse bavette = `transversus-abdominis` = US inside skirt** — trade
> "Inside skirt (Transversus abdominis)." — <https://archive.ahdb.org.uk/inside-skirt-transversus-abdominis>

UNECE FR lists *flanchet* as dividing into "bavette de flanchet, fausse bavette et bavette
d'aloyau" → "flank steak, inside skirt and internal flank plate"
(<https://mymemory.translated.net/en/French/English/flanchet>). **Flanchet itself is a
primal region, not a muscle.**

**Onglet = `diaphragm-crus` (pars lumbalis, the two crura) = US hanger** — trade
> "Il correspond à chacun des deux piliers du diaphragme" — <https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onglet_(viande)>

> "pilier du diaphragme … latin: 'crus diaphragmatis' (TA) … **Il correspond à l'onglée des
> animaux.**" — <https://www.academie-medecine.fr/le-dictionnaire/index.php?q=pilier+du+diaphragme>

**Hampe = `diaphragm` (pars costalis) = US outside skirt** — trade
> "Elle correspond au diaphragme et à son attache aux côtes" — <https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampe_(viande)>

> "The pars costalis attaches in an oblique direction to the thirteenth through eighth ribs"
> — <https://veteriankey.com/the-diaphragm/>

> "shall consist of the diaphragm including the *serous membrane* (peritoneum) attached"
> — IMPS/NAMP 121C, <https://www.whatchefswant.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Outside-Skirt-00100.pdf>

⚠ UNL lumps both under `Diaphragm` — "Skirt, Outside skirt, Hanging Tender"
(<https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/53>). The French pair separates them
cleanly; **there is no onglet/hampe swap**, despite UK "thick skirt" = onglet muddying it.

### Hindquarter

**Tri-tip = `tensor-fasciae-latae`** — trade
> `Tensor fasciae latae` — Common Name: "Tri-tip, Triangle muscle, Knuckle cap"
> — <https://bovine.unl.edu/muscle-descriptions/110>; also "Tensor fascia latae | Tri-tip" (Calkins)

The confusion neighbours: `Biceps femoris` carries "**Top Sirloin Cap**" (= coulotte /
picanha, **not** tri-tip) and `Gluteus medius` carries "Top sirloin".

**Sirloin tip / knuckle = the `quadriceps-femoris` group** — trade
> "Quadriceps femoris | Knuckle; Sirloin tip" · "Rectus femoris | Knuckle center" ·
> "Vastus lateralis | Knuckle side" — Calkins & Sullivan

All four heads carry "Knuckle" at UNL (`/29`, `/31`, `/32`, `/39`). UNL also puts
"Outside round" on the quadriceps — a source quirk; reproduce, do not correct.

**Rond de gîte = `semitendinosus`** — trade
> "le Longissimus thoracis (entrecôte) et le Semitendinosus (rond de gîte)"
> — <https://journees3r.fr/textes3r/20110703-marqueurs-des-qualites-sensorielles-de-la-viande-bovine-en-race-salers/>

**Tende de tranche = `semimembranosus`** — trade
> "Semimembranosus ou tende de tranche" — <https://www.inrae.fr/actualites/biomarqueurs-proteiques-evaluer-persille-tendrete-viande-bovine>

**Gîte à la noix = `biceps-femoris`, rumsteck = `gluteus-medius`** — trade
> "six muscles grillés : le gîte noix ( biceps femoris ), le tende de tranche
> ( semimembranosus ), le faux-filet ( longissimus ), le rumsteck ( gluteus medius ), le
> paleron ( infraspinatus ) et le filet ( psoas major )" — with an EN bridge giving
> outside / topside / striploin / rump / oyster blade / tenderloin
> — <https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122439/records/669e832f00eb85b7d72c0403>

**Mouvant (dessus de tranche) = `quadriceps-femoris`, vastus medialis head** — trade
> "Partie de la tranche grasse du bœuf, correspondant au muscle vaste interne"
> — <https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2alpha/alpha-fra.html?lang=fra&index=alt&srchtxt=MOUVANT+TRANCHE+GRASSE>
> Latin bridge: "muscle vaste interne … musculus vastus medialis" — OQLF fiche 17077730

**Merlan = sartorius (muscle couturier)** — trade, **unmodelled**
> "Le merlan est un morceau de viande de bœuf à griller, constitué par le muscle couturier"
> — <https://www.gastronomiac.com/lexique_culinaire/merlan-boucherie/>
> Latin bridge: OQLF fiche 8441051, "muscle sartorius … Synonyme : muscle couturier"

**Cattle have no separately dissectible `gluteus-superficialis`.** It is fused into the
gluteobiceps complex — worth knowing before assigning it to anything:
> "the superficial gluteal muscle is additionally incorporated into this complex, losing its
> independent identity and being recognizable only by a slightly different orientation of its
> fibers" … "the gluteobiceps muscle is particularly broad in Ruminants"
> — <https://www.imaios.com/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/gluteobiceps-muscle-11078084864>

Do **not** cite IMAIOS's standalone *gluteus superficialis* entry for cattle — it gives
carnivore origin/insertion with no ruminant note.

**살치살 is settled, and it is `serratus ventralis`.** The note above says the Korean

> — 식품의약품안전처고시 제2019-113호 [별표 3]

That also makes 살치살 the same muscle as the American Denver steak and Japanese ザブトン.
The namu.wiki *spinalis* claim is unquotable (403) and is not adopted.

**`law.go.kr` is not closed.** The note above says 고시 2894 is JS-gated — that is a
*different*, 2007 농림부 고시. The current one is reachable through non-JS siblings:

- `https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoR.do?admRulSeq=2100000184120` — 본문 as plain text
- `.../admRulBylInfoR.do?admRulSeq=2100000184120` — lists the four 별표

## Traps found the hard way

**WebFetch does not return raw text.** It runs a small model over the page which
paraphrases, summarises, **merges adjacent passages** and translates out of the source
language — silently and plausibly. Three merged quotes were caught in the UK file on
review and eight assembled ones in the US file. Two agents independently stopped using
it for quotes and switched to `curl` + a programmatic substring check: Japan verified
105/105 quotes as literal substrings of saved raw page text, Russia 91/91, Brazil 75/75.
**That is the method. Use it.**

**`pdftotext` needs `-enc UTF-8`** or the default Latin-1 dump silently drops every CJK
character.

**ABIEC's multilingual glossary does not extract for CJK or Cyrillic.** The RUSSO /
RUSSIAN column header is present and the document contains zero Cyrillic; the Korean and
Chinese cells come out empty (no ToUnicode map); the Japanese column holds katakana
transliterations of the *English export* names (リブアイ, キューブロール) and contains
none of ミスジ, ザブトン, ハラミ, イチボ. The glossary rows also decouple from the
`Componentes musculares:` lines in the reading-order dump. **It is a Brazilian source
only** — the suggestion that it could bridge into JP/KR/RU was wrong.
