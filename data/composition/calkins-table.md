# Calkins & Sullivan — Common Names for Beef Muscles

Extracted verbatim from the Beef Checkoff fact sheet PDF, because it is the single most
useful page in this whole exercise and it comes back as undecoded binary through a
plain fetch. **Quote from here rather than re-fetching the PDF.**

> Source: *Ranking of Beef Muscles for Tenderness Fact Sheet.* Written by Chris Calkins
> and Gary Sullivan, University of Nebraska. Funded by The Beef Checkoff. ©2007
> Cattlemen's Beef Board and NCBA.
> PDF: <https://www.paffa.org/system/ckeditor_assets/attachments/1146/66020_Beef_Muscle_Common_Names.pdf>
> Full paper: <https://www.agrireseau.net/bovinsboucherie/documents/Ranking%20of%20Beef%20Muscles%20for%20Tenderness.pdf>

Two things to notice before leaning on it. It covers **32 muscles**, the ones they
ranked for tenderness — there is no row for the diaphragm, masseter, transversus
abdominis, obliquus externus, subscapularis, pectineus, sartorius, complexus, splenius
or longus colli, so a missing row is not evidence of absence. And it gives **English
cut names only, with no bone boundaries** — it can tell you the longissimus is both the
ribeye and the loin eye, and it cannot tell you where one stops and the other starts.
Grade anything resting on it `trade`, and take bounds from IMPS or CFIA.
