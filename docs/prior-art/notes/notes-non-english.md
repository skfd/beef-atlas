## Genuine multi-country equivalence tables — about eight exist
Almost all PDFs, almost all trade-facing, almost all undated or frozen.

**BEST BY A DISTANCE — ABIEC, Livro Brasileiro de Cortes Bovinos / Brazilian Beef Cuts Book**
https://www.abiec.com.br/wp-content/uploads/ABIEC_LIVRO-DE-CORTES_COMPLETO.pdf
(landing: https://abiec.com.br/publicacoes/livro-brasileiro-de-cortes-bovinos/)
A coded cut catalogue whose glossary is a true **12-language aligned table**: Portuguese, English,
French, Spanish, Italian, German, Arabic, Russian, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Farsi. Rows carry cut
codes — B2111 Raquete / Oyster Blade / Paleron / Marucha / Copertina / Schaufelstueck. It even encodes
*intra*-Spanish variation inline: **72 occurrences of "(Chile: ...)"**, e.g. Marucha vs
*Chile: Punta Paleta*. ~36 coded cuts recoverable. Frozen 2019/2020; ABIEC's page tells you to request
it by email while two full copies sit openly on the same server. *verified*

**Lexicool "BEEF CUTS" nine-column table** — see notes-english-cross-country.md. Nine country columns
(AR, ES, BR, CL, PT, USA/UK, FR, DE/CH, IT), ~200 cells, created 5 Sep 2003, unattributable.

**MAGyP (Argentine agriculture ministry), Glossary of Main Export Cuts**
https://www.magyp.gob.ar/sitio/areas/bovinos/informacion_interes/informes/_archivos//000018_Nomencladores/000000-%20Presentaci%C3%B3n%20glosario%20de%20cortes%20bovinos,%20porcinos%20y%20ovinos.pdf
Nine columns — Argentina, Spain, Brazil, Chile, Portugal, USA/UK, France, Germany/Switzerland, Italy
— beef, offal, lamb, pork, ~40-50 beef rows. Source file "Glosario de cortes vacunos 2007.doc".
*verified*

> country set in the same order**. Cell contents were not diffed, so this is not asserted as fact —
> but they are very likely the same underlying table, one 2003 and one 2007. If so, the "widest
> Spanish-language matrix" and the "richest multilingual PDF" are one document, and the real count of

**Weidefleisch.org, Register der Teilstuecke vom Rind** | https://weidefleisch.org/hilfsmittel/teilstuecke-rind/
~60 cuts keyed by numeric code (1102-1514), each tagged **de, at, ch, us, uk, plus fr and it** —
structurally a cross-equivalence register, not a glossary with translations sprinkled in. Verbatim:
`1210 = Hueftdeckel (de), Tafelspitz (at), Cap of rump (uk), Codone (it), Culotte d'aiguillette
baronne (fr)`. No copyright year, no last-updated date. **The closest German-language prior art to the
CarneAtlas concept.** *verified*

**Swatland 2012** and **Aussie Beef & Lamb AU<->US code chart** — see notes-english-cross-country.md.

**it.wikipedia, Tagli di carne bovina** | https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagli_di_carne_bovina
~40 Italian cuts plus dedicated sections for USA (8), UK (13), Germany (14), France (30), Argentina
(31), Brazil/Portugal (16), Turkey (12). Last edited 7 Jan 2026. **The best single *free*
multi-country cut list found in any language** — but bulleted lists per country, not a unified matrix.
*verified*

- **Mapa da Carne, Nomes Regionais** https://www.mapadacarne.com.br/nomes-regionais — 70+ entries

## French — and a correction
- **fr.wikipedia, Decoupe du boeuf** https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9coupe_du_b%C5%93uf — nine
  national systems, French section 29 cuts. But it **stacks nine independent diagrams with zero
  equivalence rows**, and the Argentine section is empty. Self-flagged as under-sourced.
- **Bloc-Notes Culinaire** https://www.bloc-notes-culinaire.com/2017/02/traduction-des-decoupes-de-viandes-de.html
  The best genuine FR->EN **table**: 100+ beef cuts plus pork and poultry. Flags that Quebec
  francophones use literal American calques, then declines to tabulate them. Feb 2017, unrevised.
- **Connexion France** https://www.connexionfrance.com/practical/explained-french-cuts-of-beef/391041
  ~30+ FR cuts to UK equivalents grouped by cooking method; explains the differing carcass division.
  2020, **modified June 2026** — most current anglophone item.
- Institutional French: **la-viande.fr** (Interbev consumer arm, active); **viandes-rhd.fr posters**
  https://www.viandes-rhd.fr/ressources-techniques-viandes/posters-de-decoupe-de-carcasses-et-morceaux/
  (PDF charts, updated 2023 — fresher than Interbev's own copy); **Interbev's denomination accord**
  https://www.interbev.fr/ressource/accord-relatif-a-la-denomination-des-morceaux-de-viande-de-bovin-veau-et-dovin-en-libre-service/
  (regulatory backbone, 2014 agreement / 2015 decree). **Not one institutional French source offers a
  single English equivalent.**
- **CORRECTION: France does not have the regional naming variation the brief assumed.** Every source
  was checked specifically for it and none was found. What France has instead is a **two-register
  split** — traditional butcher names (macreuse, paleron, aiguillette baronne) versus simplified
  supermarket labels (steak, roti, bourguignon) plus 1-3 star tenderness ratings, imposed by a July
  2014 decree, artisanal butchers exempted, contested in the Senate in 2015
  (https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2015/qSEQ150214744.html). La-viande.fr names both registers but
  **publishes no cut-by-cut crosswalk between them. That missing crosswalk is the real French content
  gap, not geography.**
- Francophone Canada is a **third, separate French naming dialect** built on American calques that no
  France-based source cross-references (https://fr.canadabeef.ca/coupes-par-couleur/, (c)2018, stale).
- **Trap:** `agriculture.gouv.fr/telecharger/88262` looks like cut nomenclature. Extracted locally —
  it is the "Plan de la filiere viande bovine francaise", a policy document on Label Rouge targets and
  carbon, with **no cut names at all. Do not cite it.**

## German — the divergence is institutional and well documented
- **de.wikipedia, Teilstuecke von Rindfleisch** https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teilst%C3%BCcke_von_Rindfleisch
  German section on DLG cutting rules (~18-20 cuts), Austrian section in seven groups, Swiss section.
  States *why* names diverge: Germany follows Deutsche Landwirtschafts-Gesellschaft rules, Austria has
  the **Wiener Teilung** running in parallel. Last edited 20 Sep 2025.
- **Wiener Teilung** https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Teilung — the Austrian scheme, rooted in
  Vienna's 1873 Qualifikationstabelle that split beef into 22 parts. Provenance for why AT != DE.
- **GuteKueche.at** https://www.gutekueche.at/rindfleisch-artikel-1917 — 17 Austrian cuts explicitly
  contrasted against German names.
- **culinamus** https://culinamus.com/en/3-knowledge/beef-cuts/ — a real four-column table: German /
  culinary use / English / French, 12 cuts, Austrian/Viennese standards. Undated.
- **Germany vs Austria is real and evidenced.** Weidefleisch gives it row by row: Hochrippe (de) =
  Rostbraten (at) = Hohruecken (ch) = Ribeye (us) = Forerib (uk); **Hueftdeckel (de) = Tafelspitz
  (at)**. GuteKueche adds Nuss (AT) = Rinderzapfen (DE). **Swiss German is a genuine third column**,
  not a rounding error: the forequarter is *Bug* in Vienna but *Laffe* in Swiss German, and
  Proviande's names don't map to German ones.
- **Unmet demand:** Germany's go-to English resource is still the 24 January **2011** hobby blog post
  at https://www.noordinaryhomestead.com/butcher-shop-cheet-sheet-auf-deutsch/ — 65 comments running
  through September 2024, author still replying. A 15-year-old blog post is the state of the art for
  US<->Germany.

## Italian — regional variation is real, and worse than "regional"
- **I nomi della carne** https://www.inomidellacarne.it/denominazione-e-comparazione-dei-tagli-di-carne-regionali/
  and .../mancate-corrispondenze/ — **the one site in any language that treats regional cut-name
  divergence as its subject.** Comparison table across five regions (Sicily, Puglia, Lazio, Lombardy,
  Piedmont), with Sciacca as a fieldwork site. Honest about its holes: "Manca la regione Toscana
  poiche manca anche nei manuali."
- **it.wikipedia per-cut articles** https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girello_(taglio_di_carne) — cleanest
  regional evidence: girello is *Rotondino* in Genoa, *Magatello* in Milan, *Coscia rotonda* in Turin,
  *Lacerto* in Naples and Palermo.
- **Braciamiancora regional dictionary** https://www.braciamiancora.com/il-dizionario-regionale-dei-tagli-bovini/
  + English mirror https://grillexperience.it/en/dictionary-bovine-cuts/ — compiled with butchers
  across 11 regions. Data embedded as **image tables** — not machine-readable. 2018.
- **Casa Mia Tours** https://casamiatours.com/italian-beef-cuts-translated/ — best anglophone-facing
  Italian item, ~15+ cuts IT->US/UK, foregrounds regional variation. 2017 but **updated 26 Sep 2025** —
  the only genuinely revised item in the Italian set.
- Also verified: **Le Bonta de' Beccai Fiorentini**
  https://www.lebontadebeccaifiorentini.it/it/tagli-della-carne-bovina.html (a Florentine butchers'
  collective — primary-source Tuscan voice, valuable precisely because Tuscany is missing from the
  manuals); **Accademia Macelleria Italiana**
  https://accademiamacelleriaitaliana.it/nomi-tagli-di-carne-del-bovino/; **Carne Genuina**
  https://www.carnegenuina.it/blogs/guida-di-cucina/i-tagli-di-carne-italiani-e-nomi-regionali
  (15 cuts against ~30 cities, 2019).

**Two Italian findings that should change any data model:**
1. **The collisions run both ways.** *Cappello del prete* means only the terminal part of the dorsal
   muscle in Turin but the whole muscle in Sciacca; *Osso buco* is the inner thigh in Milan but the
   rear hock in Sciacca; *Fesa* is outer thigh in Bari but inner thigh in Sciacca. **A name->cut
   lookup is not a function, and a naive many-to-one schema will silently return wrong answers.**
2. **The sources contradict each other** — Carne Genuina puts *rotolo di coscia* in Milan where
   Wikipedia puts *Magatello* — and the unit of variation is arguably the **city**, not the region.
   Any Italian data needs a per-claim source field. The one reported invariant: *filetto* means the
   same thing everywhere.

## Where coverage is genuinely thin
**The US-only search bias did not bite as predicted.** Across Spanish (9 queries), Portuguese (8),
French (8), German (4) and Italian (5), essentially none came back US-only — because the query strings
are themselves ES/PT/FR/DE/IT words with no English homographs, so native domains surfaced anyway.
Junk rate 1-2 queries per language; the one clear case of US skew was an Italian query about bistecca
alla fiorentina.

What actually limited coverage was **fetch failures and unreadable formats**:
- **Fetch-blocked:** unece.org (403 everywhere), ProZ.com (403 — so the CMA 13-language glossary could
  not be confirmed; publisher CMA was dissolved, probably dead), Eurodicas (403), agrarschulen.at
  (403), toytowngermany.com (refused), COMECARNE's PDF (site rebuilt, file gone, Wayback rate-limited),
  the French ministry's BO-Agri decoupe guide (connection refused twice). **reddit.com and
  seriouseats.com are blocked to this user agent**, so forum demand is genuinely unmeasured.
- **Data trapped in images and PDFs:** Gut Streitdorf's Austrian table is a PNG, Braciamiancora's
  Italian regional dictionary is image tables, IUMM's Spanish table is JS-loaded, and the image-heavy
  ABIEC edition's glossary pages are not text-extractable. **Almost nothing in any language is
  machine-readable.**
- **Dates are mostly worthless.** Many sites carry an auto-renewing "(c) 2026" with no real revision
  date. Across the entire Italian set exactly one item (Casa Mia Tours) shows a verifiable recent
  revision.
- **Incomplete rather than empty:** Mexico (no verified institutional source), Colombia (one news
  article), Portugal (one Wikipedia section, one consumer explainer), Spanish Wikipedia (no such
  article at all), and Ireland, Canada, Poland and the Nordics (nothing found). Switzerland and the
  Netherlands: one hobbyist page each. Japan and Korea drown in wagyu marketing, with Swatland 2012
  the only real mapping. **Pork and lamb are an afterthought everywhere**, despite diverging more than
  beef across borders.

## Verdict
**Barbell-shaped with nothing in the middle.** At one end, codified trade standards and export
glossaries (ABIEC's 12 languages, the nine-column Lexicool/MAGyP table, AUS-MEAT/HAM, the Aussie Beef
AU-US chart) are accurate, sometimes genuinely multilingual, and share a code spine — but they are
PDFs written for exporters, frozen between 2003 and 2020, several 403-walled or served only on
request. At the other end, single-country national nomenclators and one-off expat blog posts.

The **consumer-facing, browsable, many-country mapper barely exists**: Cutranslator holds the
interactive slot with broad-but-programmatic data and no imagery, Meat My Nation is an empty MVP, and
the only per-region tabulations for Brazil and Mexico are on an anonymous site and a distributor's
blog respectively.

**So the equivalence data has been compiled repeatedly, well, and by institutions — it is just locked
in unattributed PDFs and trade documents in languages that don't cross-reference each other. The
consumer product on top of it is not built. That, rather than the raw mapping, is what's unoccupied.**
